## Relevant Industry Standards or Protocols

- **Export Administration Regulations (EAR)** — US BIS rules governing dual-use goods; ECCN classification is central to platform workflows
- **International Traffic in Arms Regulations (ITAR)** — US State Department controls on defence articles; USML classification required
- **OFAC Sanctions Lists** — Office of Foreign Assets Control lists that form the core of denied party screening
- **EU Dual-Use Regulation (2021/821)** — European equivalent of EAR governing export of dual-use items
- **Harmonized System (HS) / Schedule B** — international product classification used for duty calculation and export documentation
- **AES (Automated Export System)** — US CBP electronic filing required for exports above de minimis value
- **WCO SAFE Framework** — World Customs Organization standards for trusted trader and customs-to-customs data exchange

## Market Research

**Market Size:** The trade compliance software market is valued at approximately USD 3.09 billion in 2025, projected to grow significantly through 2029 driven by expanding sanctions regimes, new tariff structures, and increasing regulatory scrutiny of supply chains.

**Funding / M&A:** Consolidation is accelerating. WiseTech Global acquired e2open (which includes Amber Road) for USD 2.1 billion in August 2025. Descartes has made multiple acquisitions including Visual Compliance and OCR Global EASE. Thomson Reuters continues to invest in ONESOURCE.

**Pricing Landscape:** All major platforms use custom enterprise pricing. Entry-level configurations for SMBs start around USD 5,000–15,000 per year. Mid-market deployments with ERP integration typically cost USD 50,000–200,000 per year. Large enterprise full-suite implementations can reach seven figures.

**Key Buyer Personas:** Export compliance officers and trade compliance managers at manufacturers exporting controlled goods; customs managers at importers handling complex tariff engineering; legal and regulatory affairs teams at defence and aerospace companies; supply chain directors managing multi-jurisdiction trade flows.

**Notable Trends:** Geopolitical fragmentation — US-China trade tensions, Russian sanctions, new Middle East restrictions — is driving demand as companies need to screen broader counterparty universes more frequently. Tariff engineering and duty optimisation are growing alongside compliance screening. AI-powered ECCN classification is reducing the expert bottleneck. Carbon border adjustment mechanisms (CBAM) in the EU are adding a new compliance layer. Small and mid-size exporters are demanding affordable SaaS alternatives to legacy enterprise platforms.

## AI-Native Opportunity

- AI-assisted ECCN and HS code classification using product descriptions and technical specifications, reducing reliance on scarce export control specialists for routine classifications
- Continuous sanctions monitoring — alerting compliance teams to counterparty risk changes between transaction-time screenings, not just at point of booking
- Natural-language regulatory change summaries — automatically digesting new BIS, OFAC, and EU updates and flagging which existing product lines or customer relationships are affected
- Automated licence determination — given a product, destination country, and end-user type, predicting whether a licence is required and pre-populating the application
- Supply chain counterparty graph analysis — surfacing indirect exposure to sanctioned entities through multi-tier supplier and distributor relationships
